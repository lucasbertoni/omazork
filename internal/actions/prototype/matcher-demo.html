The question this demo answers: the wrapper sees only the player's raw command and the game's reply (plus room and move counters). From that alone it must decide, every turn, how long the player waits to see the outcome: instantly (combat, quick glances, fumbles), a walk's worth of minutes (movement between rooms), or an action's worth ((verb, object) rows). Click through the transcripts below — every Zork I and Zork III turn is a real, seeded run of the actual engine — and judge: does the classification ever feel wrong?

Durations come from small hand-written demo tables standing in for the generated ones. The Zork II tab is reconstructed from verbatim game text (the dragon lives too deep to script quickly); it is labeled as such.
